The Scenario

Welcome to SuperValue! As a governance advisor, you've been called in to assist the executive leadership with a critical decision. SuperValue, a leading UK supermarket chain, is considering deploying Facewatch live facial recognition technology in 50 of its high-crime locations. The system aims to identify individuals associated with theft, violence, or aggression by comparing customer facial images against a watchlist, alerting staff to potential threats. The Chief Operating Officer, who is warm, reassuring, and optimistic about the potential benefits, will be your main point of contact. SuperValue believes this system could significantly reduce theft, enhance staff safety, boost operational efficiency, and cut financial losses. However, there are growing concerns about privacy, freedom of movement, potential biases in accuracy, due process, transparency, and compliance with data protection regulations. Your task is to navigate these competing considerations and provide a well-reasoned recommendation.
Your Goal

Conduct a structured proportionality assessment of a proposed live facial recognition (LFR) system for SuperValue, applying the Zero Questions framework (Legitimacy, Suitability, Necessity, and Balancing). Based on your assessment, provide a justified recommendation to either Proceed, Modify, or Halt the proposal, outlining any necessary safeguards or conditions.

SUPERVALUE GOVERNANCE ADVISORY - PROPORTIONALITY ASSESSMENT

PART A: INTRODUCTION

As governance advisor to SuperValue, I have conducted a structured proportionality assessment of the proposed Facewatch live facial recognition (FRT) deployment across 50 high-crime stores. My analysis applies the Zero Questions framework (Legitimacy, Suitability, Necessity, and Balancing) as a go/no-go checkpoint, guided by the principles of proportionality, do no harm, and the precautionary principle. This assessment also considers COBRA context-based risk analysis, stakeholder engagement, and the mitigation hierarchy.

SuperValue's COO has presented a case centered on staff safety, theft reduction, and operational efficiency. These are legitimate aims. However, the deployment of FRT in public-facing retail environments engages significant rights considerations under UK data protection law (UK GDPR), the Human Rights Act 1998 (Article 8 right to private life), and the Equality Act 2010. Biometric data is classified as sensitive personal data, requiring heightened protection and a clear lawful basis.

The core question is whether this intervention is proportionate - meaning it must not exceed what is necessary to achieve its legitimate aims.

PART B: ZERO QUESTIONS ASSESSMENT

1. LEGITIMACY TEST

Question: Is the purpose valid, lawful, and defensible?

Findings: The test PASSES IN PRINCIPLE but with significant legal qualifications.

Valid Aims: Reducing theft, protecting staff from violence, and improving operational efficiency are legitimate business and safety objectives.

Lawfulness: FRT involves processing biometric data, which is special category data under UK GDPR. This requires a specific lawful basis and either explicit consent or substantial public interest justification. The ICO has previously raised concerns about FRT in retail, emphasizing that organizations must demonstrate necessity and proportionality. SuperValue must conduct a full Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) before proceeding.

Defensibility: While safety aims are defensible, the public acceptability of FRT in supermarkets is contested. Customer trust and brand reputation are at stake. A defensible approach requires transparency, meaningful consultation, and robust safeguards.

Key Glossary Terms Applied:
- Legitimacy (test): Purpose must be valid, lawful, and defensible
- Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA): Legally required for high-risk processing
- Facial Recognition Technology (FRT): Biometric system; sensitive personal data
